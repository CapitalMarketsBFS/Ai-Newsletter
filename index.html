Top Stories in BFSI AI

4 stories this fortnight
Agentic AI

AI agents are coming to your back office — and they won't wait for instructions

The next wave of AI in BFSI doesn't answer questions — it takes independent action. Agentic AI systems can plan, execute multi-step tasks, and call on tools like core banking systems and CRM platforms without a human in the loop.

Agentic AI refers to systems that can pursue goals autonomously over extended sequences of actions — not just generating text, but booking meetings, filing reports, querying databases, and triggering downstream workflows. In banking, this means an AI that can take a customer's loan inquiry, pull their transaction history, run a credit assessment, and draft a decision letter, all without human involvement.

Goldman Sachs' new AI agent platform scans earnings transcripts, financial filings, and market data to produce analyst-grade equity research notes across more than 3,000 stocks. Tasks that previously required a junior analyst several hours now complete in minutes. The platform flags decisions that require human sign-off and routes them accordingly — a model of human-AI collaboration that many BFSI firms are now studying closely.

For consultants, the key challenge is governance: who is accountable when an autonomous agent makes a consequential decision? The firms building robust human-in-the-loop controls now will have a significant advantage when regulators inevitably formalise requirements.

Trade Finance

HSBC's document AI: how GenAI is conquering the paper mountain in trade finance

HSBC's deployment of generative AI for trade finance document processing has cut cycle times by 70% — turning a historically manual, error-prone process into a near-automated operation.

Trade finance has long relied on dense paper documentation — letters of credit, bills of lading, certificates of origin. HSBC's system extracts, cross-references, and validates these documents against transaction records in real time, flagging discrepancies instantly. The result: fewer delays, lower risk of fraud, and significant cost savings per transaction at scale. The model is now being studied by other trade banks as a template for global rollout.

AI Concept of the Day

Explained for BFSI professionals
Concept · June 2026
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
noun phrase  /  artificial intelligence · large language models
A technique that combines a large language model's reasoning abilities with a live search over a firm's own documents, policies, and data — so the AI answers questions using your specific content, not just general training knowledge. In BFSI, RAG means an AI assistant grounded in your actual product terms, regulatory guidance, and internal procedures rather than guessing or hallucinating.
🏦
Why it matters in BFSI
Generic AI models don't know your firm's T&Cs, current product rates, or the specific FCA rules that apply to your customer base. RAG fixes this by giving the model access to your documents, your policies, and your data at query time — dramatically reducing hallucination risk and enabling AI assistants that are genuinely trustworthy in a regulated environment. Lloyds' branch co-pilot runs on RAG. So does Goldman's research agent.

AI Prompting Tips for BFSI

Practical techniques for this fortnight's themes
🔍 Tip 5
Ground the AI in a specific document before asking questions
Here is our current KYC policy document: [paste text]. Based only on this document, answer the following question and cite the relevant section: [your question].
Why it works: By pasting in your document and instructing the model to draw only from it, you create a manual RAG setup without any technical infrastructure. The model treats your pasted content as authoritative and cites it — significantly reducing hallucination risk for compliance-sensitive queries.
⚙️ Tip 6
Ask the AI to identify what it doesn't know before answering
Before answering, list any information you would need but don't have to give a fully accurate response. Then answer as best you can, flagging any assumptions you've made.
Why it works: In BFSI contexts — regulatory analysis, risk assessments, client scenarios — a confident wrong answer is more dangerous than a flagged uncertain one. This technique surfaces the model's knowledge gaps before they become errors, and trains you to spot what additional context to provide.
🗂️ Tip 7
Use the "before / after" prompt structure for process transformation
Here is our current [KYC onboarding / claims process / credit review process — describe it]. Redesign this process assuming an AI agent can handle any step that does not require human judgment or regulatory sign-off. Show the before and after as a numbered step list.
Why it works: This structure forces the AI to engage analytically with your actual process rather than generate generic AI transformation advice. The before/after format makes the output immediately usable in a client presentation or internal paper.
🧪 Tip 8
Red-team your AI output before sharing it
Here is a document / recommendation / analysis I've drafted with AI assistance: [paste]. Act as a sceptical senior regulator (or auditor, or risk officer). What are the three most significant weaknesses, omissions, or risks in this output that I should address before relying on it?
Why it works: AI outputs can be fluent and plausible while still being incomplete or subtly wrong. Using AI to critique AI — with a specific adversarial persona — catches issues you might miss and demonstrates due diligence. Particularly valuable for compliance documents, regulatory submissions, and client-facing advice.
⚡
The Agentic Prompt: When AI Does the Work, Not Just the Writing
Agentic prompting goes beyond asking for text — it instructs the AI to plan and execute. Try: "You are working on behalf of a compliance manager. Your goal is to produce a gap analysis between our current AML policy and the latest FATF guidance. Break this into sub-tasks, tell me what information you need from me for each, and complete the ones you can independently." This structure turns a single prompt into a mini project plan.

Case Study

Deep-dive: AI in action in BFSI
Deep-Dive Case Study
HSBC
GenAI in Trade Finance
Global Transaction Banking · Trade Finance Operations · 2025–2026
The Challenge
HSBC processes millions of trade finance transactions annually, each accompanied by dense paper documentation — letters of credit, bills of lading, certificates of origin, inspection certificates, and insurance documents. Manual review of these documents was labour-intensive, error-prone, and slow — averaging 5–7 days per transaction. Discrepancies between documents, a leading source of delays and fraud risk, were difficult to catch consistently at scale. The bank estimated that document checking alone consumed over 60% of total trade operations staff time.
The AI Approach
HSBC deployed a generative AI system trained on trade finance documentation standards (including UCP 600 and ISBP guidelines) that can extract, interpret, and cross-validate all documents in a transaction package simultaneously. The system checks for discrepancies between documents — for example, a quantity on a bill of lading that doesn't match the letter of credit — and generates a plain-English summary of any issues found. Importantly, the AI operates as a first-pass checker: human specialists review flagged exceptions and handle edge cases, while clean transactions progress automatically. The model was integrated directly into HSBC's existing trade finance platform via API, avoiding a costly systems replacement.

What Made It Work
📐
Domain training
The model was fine-tuned on trade finance standards and HSBC's own document templates — not deployed as a generic LLM.
🤝
Human-AI teaming
Rather than full automation, the system was designed as a first-pass screener, preserving specialist judgment for complex cases.
🔌
API-first integration
By integrating via API rather than replacing core systems, the bank achieved faster deployment and lower transformation risk.
💡
Consultant Takeaway
Trade finance is a powerful proof-of-concept for GenAI in BFSI precisely because the value is measurable, the documents are structured, and the risk of hallucination is manageable with the right human oversight design. Consultants advising banks on GenAI should look first for high-volume, document-heavy processes where current manual effort is large and the cost of errors is well-understood. Starting there builds the credibility and internal capability needed to tackle more complex, higher-stakes deployments later.
